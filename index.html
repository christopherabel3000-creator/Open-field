Open Field

Football you play by ear. Headphones recommended.

How to play

You play a full game against the computer. Before each snap you call a play. Then you play it live: you carry the ball on runs, you throw the passes, and on defense you are the linebacker trying to make the tackle. Everything around you is heard in 3D sound, so headphones work best. The closer a player is, the louder and clearer he sounds.

Calling a play

The game reads the first play. Move through the list and pick one. Your last call is remembered, so the list starts there next time.

  • Keyboard: up and down arrows (or left and right) to move through the list, Enter or Space to pick. Number keys pick a play directly: 1 run left, 2 run up the middle, 3 run right, 4 short pass, 5 medium pass, 6 deep pass.
  • Phone: swipe left or right to move through the list, double tap to pick.
  • S, or swipe up on the phone: score, clock, down and distance, timeouts.
  • R, or swipe down on the phone: repeat the last thing said.
  • Escape or P, or a two-finger tap on the phone: pause.

On fourth down the list adds Punt and Field goal. Near the end of a half you also get a field goal on any down, Timeout when the clock is running, and Kneel down when you are ahead.

Running the ball

You hear the quarterback call two huts, then the snap and the handoff. You start running toward the hole on your own, and the line crashes into the defense in front of you. Blocked defenders are a clatter of pads that stays in one place. Free defenders are footsteps coming at you; you only hear the three closest, so the ones that can tackle you stand out. When one is about to hit you, you hear him grunt and his gear rattle. That is your moment to make a move.

  • Left and right arrows, or swipe left and right: cut over about two yards. Hold an arrow to keep drifting.
  • Space, or tap: juke. Time it for when a tackler is right on you. It has a short recovery before you can juke again.
  • Up arrow, or swipe up: burst of speed, once per play.

Quick ticks on one side mean the sideline is close; step over it and you are out of bounds. In Settings you can also turn on a soft high blip that points toward open field.

Passing

You drop back with three receivers: one on the left, one over the middle, one on the right. When a receiver gets open, you hear a repeating two-note call from his side until he is covered again: the left receiver in your left ear, the middle one in both ears, the right one in your right ear. Each also has his own pitch, so you can tell them apart without headphones: left is lowest, middle in between, right highest. Throw while he is open. Throwing to a covered receiver is risky. The only other sound is a low beat in front of you: the pass rush. It speeds up as they close in and turns sharper in the last second. Wait too long and you get sacked.

  • Left arrow, or swipe left: throw to the left receiver.
  • Up arrow, or swipe up: throw to the middle receiver.
  • Right arrow, or swipe right: throw to the right receiver.
  • Down arrow, or swipe down: throw it away.

After a catch you are the receiver, and you run with the same controls as a running play.

Defense

Pick a defense, then play one linebacker. You face the offense, so their left is your right. You move up and back with the play on your own; you steer left and right.

  • On a run, follow the ball carrier's footsteps. Two quick clicks mean he is in reach. Press Space or tap to tackle. Miss, or dive from too far away, and you lose a step.
  • On a pass, a rising tone tells you which way the ball was thrown. Then a beacon ticks from the spot where it will come down. Slide toward it: the beacon turns higher in pitch when you are lined up. The ticks speed up as the ball arrives. Press Space or tap on that last flurry to break it up or pick it off.
  • Up arrow, or swipe up: burst of speed, once per play.

If you do not make the play, the rest of your defense does its best without you.

On a phone

Turn VoiceOver off while you play, because VoiceOver takes over the gestures. You can use your accessibility shortcut to switch it off and back on. The game's own voice reads everything during the game.

With JAWS, the field is an application area, so your keys go straight to the game.
